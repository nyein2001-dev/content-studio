### Altered / synthetic content disclosure — **answer "No"**

Checked against YouTube's live policy on **13 October 2026**.

- **Policy:** "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content", YouTube Help —
  <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491>
- **Second source:** "Improving AI labels for viewers and creators", YouTube Blog,
  27 May 2026 — <https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/>
- **Third source:** "Disclosing AI-generated content", YouTube Blog, 18 March 2024 —
  <https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/>

**What the policy requires** (paraphrased): creators must disclose content that is
*photorealistic* or *meaningfully altered* so that it **appears realistic** — making
a real person appear to say something they did not say, altering footage of a real
event or place, or generating a realistic scene that never happened.

**What it explicitly does not require** (paraphrased): disclosure is not needed for
content that is clearly unrealistic or animated, for ordinary production assistance,
or where synthetic media is unrealistic and the changes are inconsequential. An
openly animated explainer built end to end with AI tools is called out as falling
outside automatic detection.

**Why this video answers "No":** every frame is stylised vector animation. No real
person appears, no real place or event is depicted, no footage was altered, and the
voice is a stock synthetic narrator belonging to nobody rather than an imitation of a
real speaker. Nothing in the video could be mistaken for a recording of something
that happened. The fact that both the animation and the voice were produced
automatically is stated in the description regardless.

**Two things to know before you click through:**
1. Since 27 May 2026 YouTube can apply an AI label itself when its systems detect
   photorealistic synthetic content. A detection label can be changed back to "No" in
   **Studio → the video → Attributes → AI use**; only labels from YouTube's own
   generative tools or from C2PA "fully generative" metadata are permanent. This file
   carries no C2PA metadata.
2. YouTube's stated position is that a disclosure label does not affect reach or
   monetisation eligibility. If you would rather disclose anyway, change the answer
   to "Yes" — nothing else in the upload changes.
